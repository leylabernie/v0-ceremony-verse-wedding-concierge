## [Post 1: The Room Block Matrix Tool]

**Caption:**

Nobody warns you that the room block is where Indian destination weddings hurt the most.

At my family's 2023 wedding in the Riviera Maya, the resort gave us a great room-block rate — and then split the block across two buildings. Our elderly aunts and uncles got rooms a ten-minute walk from the main lobby. No elevator access for the young families. We had asked for the rate. We never asked for the building.

So I built a free tool that asks the questions your resort salesperson won't:

→ How many rooms do 120 guests actually need? (It's not 60 — traditional family distribution needs a buffer.)
→ How many of those must be accessible, lobby-level rooms for grandparents?
→ How many King rooms versus Double-Queen rooms when half your guests arrive as family units with kids?

Move two sliders, enter your guest count, and the full matrix builds itself.

It's free, it takes two minutes, and it's in our bio — **ceremonyverse.com**, under Planning Tools → Room-Block Matrix Calculator.

## [Post 2: The Baraat Planning Tool]

**Caption:**

The baraat almost didn't happen at our 2019 wedding.

The resort had a horse. What the resort did not have was a horse trained for the noise and movement of a 150-person baraat behind a dhol. We found out the day before — from the equestrian vendor, casually, like it was a small thing.

That's when I learned: resorts are built for one-night weddings. Our events ask for a 400-meter unobstructed pathway, a Haldi lawn with shade and a wash-up station, a Sangeet with sound past 10 PM, and power that isn't shared with the kitchen.

I turned every one of those lessons into a free checklist builder. Tick the events you're hosting — Ganesh Puja, Mehndi, Sangeet, Baraat, Vidai, Reception — pick your procession type, and it generates the exact resort venue requirements to get in writing before you sign.

The horse question alone is worth it.

## [Post 3: The Master Spreadsheet Guide]

**Caption:**

"Vegetarian" is not the same as Jain. And at an all-inclusive resort, that difference can unravel your entire wedding menu.

At our 2019 wedding, the resort kitchen tried hard — but they couldn't make a Jain version of every dish, because we never put the requirement in writing early enough. The Jain guests were fed. Barely, and late. I started a notebook that night of every question I wished I'd asked.

That notebook became the Master Room-Block Spreadsheet Tracker — and it's free.

One row per guest: building, floor, dietary requirement, accessibility needs. The live counts tell you how many Jain meals and elder-accessible rooms to lock in writing. And the deadlines matter more than the spreadsheet: specialty chef requests go to the resort at 180 days out, strict Jain-Satvik-Halal ingredients lock at 90 days, and the traveling vendor meal count finalizes at 30. Miss the first one and the others don't matter.

Our Catering Deadline Tracker builds those three dates from your wedding date automatically.
